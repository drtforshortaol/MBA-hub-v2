California Kelp Field Guide

Quick identification • Monterey Bay Aquarium volunteer reference

Kelp are brown algae, not true plants. They photosynthesize and create habitat, food, and shelter for marine life. Giant kelp forms the familiar underwater forest in Monterey Bay. This independent quick-reference guide is intended for learning and field interpretation, not definitive identification. Entries are alphabetized by common name.

Kelp anatomy

Holdfast: anchors kelp to rock; haptera: branching holdfast structures; stipe: stem-like support; blade: photosynthetic flattened tissue; pneumatocyst: gas-filled float; sorus (plural sori): reproductive patch; sporophyll: specialized reproductive blade; midrib: central thickened blade region.

Guide talking points

Ask visitors: “Is kelp a plant?” “What keeps giant kelp upright?” “Why do so many animals live in kelp forests?” Compare the many floats of giant kelp with the single terminal float of bull kelp.

Life cycle in brief

Large kelps are sporophytes. Reproductive patches release spores, which develop into microscopic male and female gametophytes. Following fertilization, a new sporophyte grows. Bull kelp often completes this cycle annually; giant kelp can persist for multiple years.
