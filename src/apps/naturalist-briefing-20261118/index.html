Aquarium Updates · Monterey Bay

Naturalist Briefing · Nov 12–18, 2026

On the water · Gray whales, comb jellies and phalaropes

Rough conditions limited boat time. One gray whale was seen swimming north along Asilomar. The reason for its direction is unknown.

The Tuesday trip reported a large bloom of Beroe comb jellies. Species identification stopped at the genus level. Dozens of red-necked phalaropes were seen close to shore.

Guest question · What happened to the Santa Cruz fin whale?

A likely juvenile female fin whale stranded at Cowell Beach on November 12. A necropsy on November 13 found severe emaciation, consistent with poor nutritional condition over an extended period. UC Santa Cruz reported on November 17 that the underlying cause of death was still undetermined, with samples collected for further testing.

For guests: “The whale was severely underweight. Scientists collected samples to investigate why she was in such poor condition.”

Marine heatwaves can change where prey is available and how hard whales have to work to find it. The response team had established no link between this whale’s condition and either a marine heatwave or domoic acid in its initial findings.
