Learn about algae
What is algae?

Algae span several evolutionary lineages. Many use sunlight to turn carbon dioxide and water into sugars, releasing oxygen. Microscopic algae include many phytoplankton that support ocean food webs. Larger forms, called seaweeds, range from thin films and filaments to kelp forests.

Algae live in salt water, freshwater, and moist places on land. Some microscopic algae supplement photosynthesis by taking in food, and some obtain energy entirely from organic matter.

How seaweed is built
  • Holdfast: grips rock or another surface.
  • Stipe: supports the blades and bends in moving water.
  • Blade: intercepts sunlight and takes up dissolved nutrients.
  • Float: brings blades toward the light in species that have one.

Compare giant kelp’s many small floats with bull kelp’s single large float. Seagrass is a marine flowering plant. Eelgrass has true roots, stems and leaves, and reproduces with flowers and seeds. Kelp is a seaweed with a holdfast, stipe and blades.

Brown, red, and green algae

Brown algae · algas pardas. Brown algae are mostly marine. Fucoxanthin contributes the olive-brown hue alongside chlorophyll a and c. Kelps form underwater forests. Rockweeds can form dense intertidal cover.

Red algae · algas rojas. Pigments such as phycoerythrin help many reds absorb available light. Forms include delicate branches, sheets, and calcified crusts. Some look purple, pink, or iridescent.

Green algae · algas verdes. Green algae live in marine, fresh, and moist terrestrial settings. Sea lettuce forms thin blades, while some Codium form dark, spongy cushions. Brown algae are more closely related to diatoms than to red or green seaweeds.

How algae reproduce

Depending on the species, algae can reproduce by fragmentation, spores, or sexual reproduction. In many seaweeds a diploid sporophyte makes haploid spores by meiosis. Spores grow into gametophytes, which produce gametes. Fertilization restores the diploid stage.

In kelps, the large form is the sporophyte. The gametophytes are microscopic. In many red algae, a fertilized egg develops into a carposporophyte on the female gametophyte. Its spores grow into free-living tetrasporophytes. Those produce haploid tetraspores that grow into gametophytes. Red algae lack flagellated stages.

Why algae matter

Phytoplankton supply food at the base of many marine food webs. Kelps create shelter and nursery habitat. Coralline algae build calcium carbonate structures, and some help build reefs. Certain blooms can harm other organisms.

Nori and dulse are foods. Brown algae supply alginates, and red algae supply agar and carrageenan. These compounds help thicken or stabilize foods and other products. Diatomaceous earth comes from diatoms’ silica shells and is used in filtration and other products.

Guest questions
Does kelp have roots?

A holdfast grips the rock like an anchor. Kelp takes up dissolved nutrients from surrounding seawater.

At the Kelp Touch Pool, try asking “Which part helps kelp stay attached when a wave comes through?”

What’s inside the kelp floats?

Gas gives the floats, called pneumatocysts, their buoyancy. The floats lift blades closer to sunlight. Giant kelp has many small floats, and bull kelp has one large float at the top of its stipe.

What’s the difference between algae, seaweed, and kelp?

Algae is a broad term that includes microscopic organisms. Seaweed means larger marine algae, and kelp is a type of brown seaweed.

Is seagrass a seaweed?

No. Seagrasses such as eelgrass are flowering plants that live underwater. They have true roots, stems and leaves, and produce flowers and seeds. Seaweeds are large algae. Kelp, for example, anchors with a holdfast and reproduces without flowers or seeds.

Where might I encounter algae at home?

Nori wraps many sushi rolls. Agar and carrageenan come from red algae and can help foods gel or thicken. Alginates from brown algae have similar uses.

Explore ocean changes
Warming and kelp loss

Marine heatwaves can stress kelp. After warming and increased urchin grazing, monitoring showed a loss of over 90% of bull-kelp canopy off Mendocino and Sonoma counties from 2014 to 2016.

Acidification and local pH

Seawater pH falls as the ocean absorbs carbon dioxide. Kelp (a brown seaweed) and eelgrass (a marine flowering plant) both take up dissolved carbon dioxide during photosynthesis, which can raise pH in nearby water. The size and duration of the effect depend on local conditions.

Ask “How could a meadow change the water flowing through it?”

Oxygen loss

Warmer water can hold less dissolved oxygen. Warming can also raise oxygen demand in animals and microbes. Low oxygen can make it harder for some animals to thrive.

Other pressures and what helps

Storms can damage habitat. Rising seas can change light and substrate availability. Runoff and sediment can affect young kelp. Introduced algae or grazers can change local communities. Protecting water quality, monitoring kelp forests, and restoring suitable habitat can support recovery. Reducing greenhouse gas emissions addresses the drivers of warming and acidification.

Compare the habitats

Kelp forests grow on rocky seafloors. Their canopy and holdfasts create shelter at different heights.

Eelgrass meadows are made up of flowering plants rooted in shallow sediment. Their blades provide cover for young fish and habitat for small invertebrates.

Go deeper
Seaweeds versus flowering plants

Seaweeds are large algae. Many have a holdfast, stipe and blades. Seagrasses are flowering plants adapted to salt water. Like flowering plants on land, they have true roots, stems and leaves, specialized transport tissues, and flowers that produce seeds. Both groups photosynthesize.

Seaweed life cycles

In many seaweeds, a spore-producing sporophyte (two chromosome sets) alternates with a gamete-producing gametophyte (one set). In kelp, the large form you see is the sporophyte. The gametophytes are microscopic.

Many red algae, including Polysiphonia, have a third stage. After fertilization, a carposporophyte develops on the female gametophyte and releases spores that grow into tetrasporophytes. Those produce spores that grow into new gametophytes.
